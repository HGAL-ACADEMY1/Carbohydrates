Types of Carbohydrates

Did you know there isn't just one type of carbohydrate? There are actually two main types.

They are:

  • 1️⃣ Simple Carbohydrates
  • 2️⃣ Complex Carbohydrates

Let's start with Simple Carbohydrates. These are very easy for humans and animals to digest. It’s like eating a grape—your body breaks it down super fast! ⚡

Examples of simple carbohydrates are Monosaccharides and Disaccharides. Big words, right? Let me explain.

"Mono" means one. So, Monosaccharides are single sugar units. The most important one you need to remember is Glucose.

🧠 Fun Fact: Glucose is needed every single second by your brain! Your brain is a hungry machine that runs on glucose.

Other examples of monosaccharides are Fructose (found in fruits 🍎) and Galactose.

Okay, let’s now move on to Disaccharides. "Di" means two. These are made of two molecules of sugar stuck together.

Here are three famous ones:

🍬 Sucrose: This is normal table sugar. When your body breaks it down, it turns into Fructose and Glucose.

🌾 Maltose: When broken down, it splits into two molecules of Glucose.

🥛 Lactose: This is popularly referred to as milk sugar. When digested, it yields Glucose and Galactose.

Now that we understand the simple ones, let’s talk about Complex Carbohydrates.

These are called Polysaccharides. Imagine a long train with many cars attached together. 🚂🚃🚃🚃

Polysaccharides contain as many as 2000 simple carbohydrate units in long chains! That is huge!

Examples of these complex giants are:

1. Starch: Found in seeds, grains, and tubers (like yams).

2. Glycogen: This is a form of glucose storage in animals.

3. Cellulose: Found in plants.

Functions of Carbohydrates

So, why do we eat them? What do they actually do for us? Let’s look at their jobs. 🛠️

⚡ 1. Body Fuel: It supplies energy which serves as fuel for both internal activities (like your heart beating) and external activities (like running or dancing).

🛡️ 2. It Spares Protein: This is a cool one! It spares protein from being used as a source of energy. Think of protein as "building blocks" for your muscles. If you don't eat enough carbs, your body burns the blocks instead! We want to keep the blocks for growing.

🔢 The Math: One gram of carbohydrate will supply 4 kilocalories of energy to the body.

🧠 3. Brain Power: As we mentioned before, it supplies glucose to the body, especially the brain.

🔥 4. Fat Burner: It actually helps the body to burn body fat efficiently.

😋 5. Flavour: It adds flavour to our diet. Imagine food without any sweetness or starchiness!

🧬 6. Genetic Helper: It helps in the formation of genetic materials. These are the blueprints of life: Deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) and Ribonucleic acid (RNA).

Digestion of Carbohydrates

Let’s now dive into what happens when you take a bite of bread. 🥪 The journey begins right in your mouth!

When you chew, the food is broken down into small pieces and mixed with your spit (saliva). Your saliva contains a special enzyme called salivary amylase (or ptyalin).

This enzyme starts cutting up the disaccharides, such as maltose, right there in your mouth.

The digestion continues in the Duodenum (which is the first part of the small intestine). Here, it meets pancreatic amylase.

This enzyme continues to break down other disaccharides into the final end product. Do you remember what the end product usually is? Yes, it is Glucose! 🎯

Next, we look at the specific enzymes in the intestine that finish the job:

🔹 Maltase: Breaks down maltose into two molecules of glucose.

🔹 Lactase: Breaks down lactose into one molecule of glucose and one of galactose.

🔹 Sucrase: Converts sucrose into one molecule of glucose and one of fructose.

⚠️ Important Note: Remember that the ultimate end product of carbohydrate digestion is glucose.

Recommended Allowances

At this point, it’s important to consider how much we should eat.

Since carbohydrate is universally available (it's everywhere!), there is no specific rule or "basis" for the formulation of a dietary allowance for it.

However, keep in mind that carbohydrates and fats are the main suppliers of energy.

🤰 You need more energy during specific times, such as pregnancy, lactation (breastfeeding), and infancy (when you are a baby).

Food Sources of Carbohydrates

Finally, let's look at where we can find these carbohydrates in our kitchen. 🥣

The major carbohydrates are found in:

🍭 Sugars

🌾 Cereal grains: Corn, millet, wheat, rice, guinea corn, and acha.

🥜 Legumes: Beans, soya beans, and groundnut.

🍠 Others: Dried fruits, tubers (yams/potatoes), and vegetables.
